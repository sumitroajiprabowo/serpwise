# content: helpful-content and E-E-A-T review

**Input:** a page (URL, file or text) or a content plan. Rules: `references/fundamentals.md` §4. For anything produced at scale, also `references/spam-policies.md`.

## Steps
1. Read the whole piece. Identify the audience, the search intent, and what a satisfied reader would have learned.
2. **Score it against Google's self-assessment questions:**
   - Is the information original?
   - Is the topic covered completely?
   - Does it add value beyond its sources?
   - Is the headline honest?
   - Is expertise evident, and are sources cited?
   - Is the author clear?
   - Are there factual errors?
3. **Check people-first signals vs search-engine-first warning signs:**
   - first-hand experience;
   - a clear site focus;
   - no writing to a word count;
   - no trend-chasing outside the site's expertise;
   - no fake freshness.
4. **Check Who / How / Why:**
   - bylines and author pages, with no fabricated authors;
   - disclosure of AI or automation where readers expect it;
   - helping people as the primary purpose.
   - if AI wrote any of it: facts checked, and the title, meta description, structured data and alt text reviewed too (`references/fundamentals.md` §4).
5. **Assess spam risk:** scaled content abuse, doorway pages, keyword stuffing, site reputation abuse.
6. **Output:**
   - strengths;
   - gaps by priority, each with a concrete edit (sections to add, claims needing evidence or sources, author info, outdated parts to update or remove);
   - an improved outline if useful.
   Rewrite the whole piece only if asked.

## Content and AI search features
Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special optimization: they draw on the same index, so the review above already covers them (`references/appearance.md` §13). When the user asks about "AEO" or "GEO":
- Point to what Google says helps: non-commodity content with first-hand experience, clear organization for people, and supporting images or video.
- Correct the myths instead of applying them: no llms.txt, no "chunking" into small pieces, no rewriting for AI or chasing every query variant, and no one page per query (that is scaled content abuse, `references/spam-policies.md`).

## Don't
- Set word-count or keyword-density targets.
- Call E-E-A-T a direct ranking factor. It describes what Google's systems reward; it isn't a score.

## Done when
Strengths, prioritized content gaps with concrete edits, and any spam or AI-content risks are reported against `references/fundamentals.md` §4 without inventing length or density targets.
